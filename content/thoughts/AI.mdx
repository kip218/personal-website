For a long time I was pretty skeptical of AI, especially for coding. When I first started using ChatGPT for scripts, they'd work fine on their own. But the second you tried using them inside a real project, they fell apart. They had no idea what was around them.

That changed when I started using Claude Code. I could use it inside my actual repos and it understood the whole codebase, not just whatever I had open in the chat. That was the thing that actually won me over.

I'm still figuring out how to use it well. Context window management is something I'm actively learning. But I'm pretty sold that this is just going to be part of how I work from here on out.

---
I'm a very skeptical person - especially when it comes to technologies that sound too good to be true. So in 2025 when everyone was really hyping up AI and the age of AI coding, I wasn't convinced.

I had tried messing around with it here and there, but the performance gains of using AI didn't really meet my expectations that would justify picking it up as a part of my stack. It required a lot of babysitting - giving it the right context, explaining what I wanted exactly, and going back and forth several times to steer it the way I wanted. It was much faster to just implement things myself.

Then I came across Claude Code in early 2026. I quickly adopted it into my daily stack and I've been obsessed ever since.
