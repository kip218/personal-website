I recently watched a Hank Green video where he talked about how in recent years, AI has accelerated the rate at which information is generated online, and thus *attention* has become a scarcer resource than the wealth of information that exists out there. In short, there is way more content online than there are people to consume it, and this will only become truer as time goes on.

For me - currently I'm juggling a full time job, a full time masters in data science, and also balancing several personal projects, which has actually been taking up most of my mind recently. I can only really juggle so much of this in my head because of AI. Claude allows me to get assignments done faster, tasks organized more effectively, and delegate work where possible. For many of my personal projects, I'm able to focus on the high level design and architecture and spend less time on the low level debugging.

That also got me thinking, that while yes, AI is helping me juggle a lot of things and now I am able to do more with less time, the real bottleneck now is my mental capacity and the time I have available. Claude can help summarize things and generate a lot of output, but I still need to read it and spend time with it to internalize it. In the end, as Hank says in his video, information is now more abundant than ever, and my time and attention has become the scarcer resource and therefore the bottleneck in my productivity.

One way I've seen this affect me at work - before LLMs and Claude, I used to context switch maybe a couple times during a work day. I could reserve blocks of time to dedicate to a specific scoped task. Now with Claude, I context switch every couple minutes, letting Claude work on one thing and then switching to the next and coming back to it once I have the mental capacity. It *feels* like I'm getting more done with less time, but also, I noticed that I've become so much more scatterbrained than ever.
