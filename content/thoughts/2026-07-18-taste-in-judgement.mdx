I've been using a lot of AI in my workflows recently.

I'm working on several work projects. I'm working on a mobile app and a couple other personal projects as well.

In all of these, I've been heavily using AI, specifically Claude. And doing so has gotten me to think of where AI is headed and what work done by humans will look like in the future.

I see a lot of strong opinions, and many people respond defensively when it comes to AI and the parts of human work that can be automated away. I understand the concerns with AI, and it's definitely become a huge buzzword that I can imagine many of the decisions made now based on an overly-optimistic view on AI will eventually come to bite us in the end.

However, for me and my own experience working with AI, it's a real time saver - and oftentimes it brings things to my attention that I would have missed otherwise. There are certain parts of my workflow that just doesn't need to be done manually anymore, and for me that's a huge plus.

Am I worried about AI taking over our jobs? No - not really. There's something I heard recently that really resonated with me quite a bit. AI can do a lot of things now, but it's only a tool. It's true that we can now do more with less, but the one thing that it can't replace is **taste in judgement**.
